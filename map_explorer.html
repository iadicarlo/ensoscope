What am I looking at?

A teleconnection is the tendency for El Niño or La Niña in the Pacific to shift conditions far away. Each map shows, for the chosen ENSO phase and season, how a rainfall, drought or heat indicator typically departs from the neutral-season average, built by compositing every past event of that phase. Set the source, season, phase and hazard below: teal areas are wetter and brown drier than usual; blue areas are cooler and red hotter.

Notes

What each hazard shows. Wet is the heaviest 10-day rainfall (Rx10day), a flood-risk sign. Drought is the longest run of dry days (CDD). Heat stress uses WBGT, a "feels-like" heat number combining temperature and humidity (high means dangerous heat for the body), and UTCI, a similar comfort index.

Anomaly vs absolute. "Anomaly" is the difference from the neutral-season average, so it shows just the El Niño / La Niña effect. That average is the mean of past neutral seasons, one per neutral period, chosen by the same full-season rule as the El Niño and La Niña seasons, so like is compared with like. The Event replay and the country packs compare with normal instead, the average of every neutral month, so their numbers differ slightly. "Absolute" is the raw value during that phase. Robustness dots appear in the anomaly view only. They answer "did past events agree on the direction of the change?", and the absolute view shows no change, only the average level during that phase, so there is nothing for the events to agree or disagree about.
